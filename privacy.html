MCPOTA Privacy Watch Privacy Policy

Effective October 17, 2026 · App ID com.mcpota.privacywatch · Published by MCPOTA (mcpota.com) · Arx Dominii Privati

This policy covers the MCPOTA Privacy Watch app for Android ("Privacy Watch", "the app"). "We" and "us" mean MCPOTA, its publisher.

The short version
  • Privacy Watch runs entirely on your phone. There is no account, no sign-in, and no Privacy Watch server.
  • We do not collect, receive, sell or share your data. Nothing you see in the app is sent to us.
  • There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking tools in the app.
  • Something leaves your phone only when you choose to send it, for example by tapping Email report or Send logs to MCPOTA support. You see the email before it's sent.

What the app does

Privacy Watch shows you which apps on your phone access your location, microphone, camera, contacts, text messages, call log, calendar, clipboard and photos, which internet addresses they look up, and how much data they send and receive in the background. It reports what was observed, not what an app did with what it accessed: it never sees the content of app traffic. It then helps you change permissions or remove apps you don't want.

For apps MCPOTA has reviewed, the app also shows what the app's privacy policy allows the company to do with your data, with the policy's own words. Grades are MCPOTA's analysis of the policy's quoted text under published rules, not a finding about the company's conduct. The whole policy database ships inside the app, so looking an app up never contacts anyone. Where a reviewed policy makes a specific promise, such as that the app doesn't access your microphone, Privacy Watch compares that promise with what it observed on your phone and shows a possible policy breach if they don't match. That comparison also happens only on your phone; nothing about it is sent anywhere.

While monitoring, Privacy Watch can show alerts as notifications on your phone when an app you installed accesses something sensitive while you're not using it, sends data shortly after, or does something its reviewed privacy policy says it doesn't. Alerts are made on your phone from the same information described below; nothing about them is sent anywhere. You choose which kinds alert in Settings → Notifications → Alerts, and Android's notification settings can turn them off.

The app records that an app used a permission or contacted an address, and when. Privacy Watch records how much each app sends, not what it sends. It does not read the content of your messages, calls, calendar, contacts, photos, files, clipboard, notifications, recordings or web pages. It does not read motion sensors either; apps can read those without a permission, and Android doesn't record which app did, so Privacy Watch can't show it.

The local VPN

Android shows a key icon while Privacy Watch is monitoring because the app uses Android's VPN feature. This VPN:

  • runs on your phone only, and does not route your traffic through any outside server;
  • handles only DNS lookups (the step where an app turns a name like example.com into an address). All your other traffic goes directly to the internet as usual;
  • passes each lookup on to your network's own DNS servers, the same servers your phone uses without Privacy Watch, and nowhere else. They see the name being looked up, just as they would without Privacy Watch;
  • cannot see the content of web pages or app traffic.

Optional advanced access

Some features need a higher level of access that Android only grants through Wireless debugging (or the Shizuku app). If you set this up, Privacy Watch connects to your own phone over that local connection. It uses the connection to:

  • read permission-use history;
  • refresh Android's per-app network counters every 30 seconds while monitoring, so it can record how much each app sent and when (amounts only);
  • make changes you ask for and agree to (see the next section), and switch Usage access on for Privacy Watch itself;
  • run Banking mode (see the next section).

Nothing obtained this way leaves your phone.

When information leaves your phone

Only when you take one of these actions:

  • Email report: the app opens your email app with the report attached. The report lists app names, domains and activity from the period you chose. You choose the recipients and press Send yourself. After that, the email is handled by your email provider and the people you send it to.
  • Send logs to MCPOTA support: after telling you what's in it, the app opens your email app with a log attached, addressed to support@mcpota.com. It contains your phone model and Android version, the app's status, the list of apps on your phone (package names, whether each came with the phone, and which store installed it) and which of them accessed the microphone, camera, location, contacts, texts, call log, calendar or other data and when, background data use, how much each app sent and when (amounts only), fixes made, and the error and crash logs. If advanced access is on, it also includes Android's recent records of microphone use, the camera service's log of when the camera was opened and closed, and Android system log lines filtered to Privacy Watch, crashes and the VPN. It contains no messages, photos, passwords, call content or browsing history. You read the email and press Send yourself. MCPOTA stores the log privately, uses it only to diagnose and fix problems (including an automated diagnosis by Anthropic's Claude AI, under terms that don't allow it to be used for training), never copies your email address alongside it, and deletes it within 30 days.
  • Report a false alarm: see "False-alarm reports" below.
  • Copy: copying logs puts them on your clipboard so you can paste them where you choose.
  • Check for updates (test builds only, not the Google Play version): after you allow it and type a tester access code, the app asks MCPOTA's builds server (run on Cloudflare) whether a newer test build exists each time you open it, and downloads it only when you tap Update now. It sends the access code and the request, nothing the app recorded. Cloudflare sees your internet address, as any website does. Android's own installer asks you to confirm. The downloaded file is deleted the next time the app opens. Turn it off in ⚙ → Turn off the update check.

False-alarm reports

If you think something was flagged by mistake, you can tap Not a concern? Report a false alarm. Privacy Watch then stops flagging it on your phone. If you also choose Hide and share, your email app opens with a short report addressed to support@mcpota.com. It contains what was flagged and facts about that app: its name, package, version, where it was installed from, the permissions it requested and was granted, and its services, plus your phone model and Android version. It contains no messages, photos, contacts, browsing or recorded activity, and you see it before pressing Send.

We use these reports only to improve how Privacy Watch detects problems. We may review them with the help of AI tools for that purpose. We don't use them for anything else, and we delete them once they've been reviewed.

Subscriptions

If you subscribe to Privacy Watch Pro, the purchase is handled by Google Play. We never see your payment card or billing details. Google Play tells the app only whether your subscription is active. Google's handling of purchases is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

What we never do

  • We don't sell or rent data, and we have none to sell.
  • We don't show ads, and we include no advertising or analytics code.
  • We don't build profiles of you or the apps you use.
  • We don't upload your activity, contacts, location or any other personal information.

Your choices

  • Stop monitoring at any time from the app, or by turning off the VPN in Android settings.
  • Delete recorded activity with ⚙ → Clear recorded events, and logs with ⚙ → Logs → Clear.
  • Undo any fix from the app (the app's timeline, or the Fixed list) whenever you like.
  • Turn off Usage access or advanced access in Android settings whenever you like.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all of its data.

Children

Privacy Watch is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age.

Security

Your data stays in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. Because nothing is sent to us, there is no server copy that could be breached.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top of this page. If a change affects what the app does with your information, we'll also explain it in the app before it takes effect.
