Startsy Privacy Policy

Startsy by MCPOTA · Android and iPhone · Effective October 27, 2026

In short

Startsy is a planner, not a privacy app. To help you, its optional AI needs to see what you're working on: the task's words and any photos or file text you add. Here is exactly what goes where.

  • Your tasks are stored only on your phone. There is no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Dictation uses your phone's own speech service. Startsy receives only the text.
  • "Snap a list" reads a photo or screenshot of a to-do list on your phone. The picture and its words are not sent anywhere, and the picture is not kept.
  • Startsy keeps a small app log on your phone (what it did and any errors, never your words). It leaves only if you send it yourself with "Send a report", from your own email app, after seeing it.
  • Test builds can check for a newer version when you open the app, only if you allow it. No task data is sent.
  • "📄 Attach a file" reads the text of a PDF or Word file on your phone and shows it to you to edit first. Only the text you keep is sent, with Break it down or Talk, after a screen that explains it. The file itself is never sent and isn't kept.
  • "Break it down" is optional. It asks you first, then sends that one task's words and any photos or file text you added to it to Startsy's AI server to get a finish line and steps back. MCPOTA doesn't store them; Anthropic, which runs the AI, deletes them within 30 days.

Information the app reads

  • What you type or dictate: each task's name, its "done when" line, its steps and which list it's in (Today, This week, Later, Done).
  • Speech recognition (only when you tap a microphone button): Startsy asks Android for microphone permission and opens a listening box that uses your phone's speech service to turn your voice into text. The mic stays on through pauses and stops when you tap Done or Cancel. On many phones that service is Google's, which may process the audio on its servers under Google's privacy policy. On iPhone, Startsy asks iOS for microphone and speech recognition permission and uses Apple's speech recognition, which runs on the phone when the iPhone supports it; otherwise Apple processes the audio under Apple's privacy policy. Startsy never keeps, stores or sends the audio; it keeps only the text. The first time, Startsy explains this and lets you type instead.

Startsy does not read your contacts, location, calendar or other apps, or your files other than one you pick yourself with 📄 Attach a file or Snap a list's 📄 Pick a file with a list. It sees only the photos you choose to add to a task or to Talk. You can take a photo with the camera or pick one. On iPhone, picked photos come from Apple's photo picker, which gives Startsy only the photos you pick, not your photo library, and camera photos aren't saved to your photo library.

Snap a list

When you tap 📷 Snap a list and take a photo or pick a picture (for example a screenshot of a task list, a whiteboard or a paper list):

  • Read on your phone. Startsy finds the words with on-device text recognition: on Android, Google ML Kit with its model built into Startsy; on iPhone, Apple's Vision framework. It works offline. The picture and its words are not sent to MCPOTA, to Startsy's AI server or to anyone else.
  • The picture is not kept. It is held in memory only while it's read, then released. On Android, a photo taken with the camera is saved briefly in Startsy's private storage (never your gallery) and deleted right after it's read; on iPhone it goes straight to Startsy and isn't saved to your photo library. Startsy sees only the one picture you choose.
  • You choose what's saved. Startsy shows every line it found before anything is added. Lines you uncheck or remove (a customer's name, anything private) are never saved. The lines you keep become ordinary tasks, stored only on your phone like any other task.
  • On Android, Startsy turns off the usage statistics ML Kit would otherwise send Google (phone model, app version, timing), so nothing about Snap a list leaves the phone.
  • 📊 From a spreadsheet (added October 27, 2026): you can also pick an Excel (.xlsx) or CSV file with your tasks in it. Startsy gets only the one file you pick from your phone's file picker, reads its first sheet on the phone with its own small reader (no other service), and shows you every task it found, just like a picture. The file and its contents are not sent anywhere, and the file isn't copied or kept.
  • 📄 From a Word, PDF or text file (added October 27, 2026): the same button, now "Pick a file with a list", also takes a Word (.docx), PDF or plain-text file. Startsy reads it on the phone with the same readers as 📄 Attach a file and makes each line of the list a task to review. Nothing is sent, and the file isn't copied or kept (on Android a PDF is copied briefly into Startsy's private storage while it's read and deleted right after).
  • 📸 Start a task from a photo (added October 29, 2026): Snap a list also has "Start a task from a photo", and when no list is found in a photo or picture, "Make a task from this photo instead". Either one puts that photo, shrunk and stripped of hidden details like any Break it down photo, into a new task form on your phone. It is held in memory only and dropped when the form closes. It is sent only if you then tap ✨ Break it down, after the same consent screens, as described under "Break it down" below.
  • From 📄 Attach a file: if the file you attach to a new task is a spreadsheet with a task column, Startsy asks whether to add each row as its own task instead. If you say yes, it goes through the same review as above.

📄 Attach a file

Added October 24, 2026. When you tap 📄 Attach a file (in Break it down or Talk mode) and pick a PDF, Word (.docx), Excel (.xlsx) or CSV file, for example your resume (spreadsheets added October 27, 2026):

  • Read on your phone. Startsy gets only the one file you pick, from your phone's file picker (Android's document picker; on iPhone, the Files app). It pulls out the file's text on the phone: on Android with Android's own PDF reader (Android 15 and later, or Android 11 to 14 with Google Play system updates; older phones can attach a Word file instead) and, for Word and Excel files, by reading the words inside the file (CSV files are plain text); on iPhone with Apple's PDFKit and the same Word and Excel readers. No other service is used.
  • You check the text first. Startsy shows you the text in a box you can edit, so you can delete anything private (your address, phone number, email, ID numbers) before anything is sent. Long files are cut to about 2 pages (6,000 characters), and Startsy says so; the rest isn't used.
  • The file isn't kept or sent. On Android the file is copied briefly into Startsy's private storage while it's read (PDFs only) and deleted right after; on iPhone it's read in place. The checked text is held in memory only, until you close the task form or Talk mode. It is never saved with your tasks.
  • What leaves your phone, and when. Only the text you checked, with that one task's words, and only when you use Break it down or Talk. The first time, a screen explains exactly what's sent and asks you. Your name in the text is replaced with a stand-in ("[name]") first. See "When information leaves your phone" below.

What the app can change on your phone

Startsy changes its own task list. When you give a task a start-by time, it also sets an alarm and shows a reminder notification on your phone at that time (with your permission to show notifications and, on Android 12 and later, to set alarms on time). It sets those alarms again after your phone restarts. Each task's reminder is your choice: a Nudge, which plays Startsy's tune once, or an Alarm, which repeats it until you answer. On iPhone, a Nudge is a Time Sensitive notification (it can come through Focus) and an Alarm is an iOS alarm (iOS 26 and later) that rings even on silent, each with your permission. If you swipe a reminder away it comes back in 10 minutes; after 3 swipes in a row it stops, and Startsy tells you the next time you open it. iOS lets an app keep only a limited number of waiting notifications, so Startsy keeps the next 64 reminders set and adds more each time you open it. You can turn all reminders off in Settings → Reminders, or remove a task's start time. Nothing else outside Startsy changes.

What is stored, and for how long

  • Your tasks are kept in Startsy's private storage on your phone until you delete them (Edit → Delete) or uninstall the app. Finished tasks stay in Done so you can undo.
  • A few settings are stored with them: whether you agreed to the dictation note, whether "Break it down" is on, whether the update check is on (with your tester access code, if you entered one), your Appearance choice (Match phone, Light or Dark), whether reminders are on, whether you last picked Nudge or Alarm (and on iPhone, the times the alarms are set for), the name you gave Startsy and its notes about how you like to work, which release notes you've already seen, whether Startsy cheers, whether you've seen the photo reminder, whether you agreed to the file-text screen, and which task is in Start here.
  • A task's start-by time is stored with the task, with Nudge or Alarm and how many times in a row its reminder was swiped away. Reminders are made on your phone; nothing about them is sent anywhere.
  • Settings (⚙) → Delete all my tasks removes every task from your phone at once.
  • A small app log for "Send a report": up to the last 300 things Startsy did and any errors, each with a time (for example "the mic started", "the camera opened", "the AI server didn't answer, status 503"). It holds only those fixed event names and numbers: never your tasks, steps, names, what you said or typed, or photos. It stays on your phone unless you send it yourself, and Settings → Send a report → Clear the log empties it. Uninstalling Startsy deletes it.
  • Startsy turns off Android's app backup, and on iPhone keeps its task file and app log out of iCloud backups, so they are not copied to cloud backups.
  • MCPOTA keeps nothing about you on its servers. The AI server does not store or log the words, file text or photos it passes on.

When information leaves your phone

"Make this step smaller" (on the Start here card) works the same way but sends only that task's name and the one step.

When you tap "Break it down for me" and have agreed on the screen that explains it:

What is sentThe words of that one task (up to 300 characters), the text you checked from an attached file if you added one (up to 6,000 characters, about 2 pages; never the file itself, with your name replaced by "[name]"), and up to 5 photos if you added them (for example your desk, the dishes or your computer screen). Photos are shrunk on your phone to about 1024 pixels and re-saved, which removes their hidden details such as location. Never your other tasks, your steps, your name or any account. The first time you include photos, Startsy reminds you to keep faces, cards, addresses, passwords and anything private out of the frame.
Where it goesMCPOTA's Startsy server, which runs on Cloudflare, passes the words and photos to Anthropic's Claude AI, which writes a finish line and steps. Cloudflare sees your internet (IP) address, as any website does, and uses it briefly to limit how many requests one network can make.
What is keptMCPOTA does not store or log the words, file text or photos, and Startsy doesn't save the photos or file text on your phone (they're dropped when you close the task form). Anthropic deletes API data within 30 days under its own policy and does not use it to train its AI (Anthropic's retention page).
How to stopSettings (⚙) → "AI breakdown is on" → tap to turn it off. Nothing is sent again unless you agree again. You can always type your own steps.

When you use Talk mode (talking with Startsy the robot) and have agreed on the screen that explains it:

What is sentWhat you say or type in Talk mode (speech is turned into text by your phone's speech service), any photos you add there (shrunk and stripped of hidden details like location, up to 5 per conversation), the text you checked from a file if you attach one (up to 6,000 characters, sent with each reply until you leave Talk mode; never the file itself), the task you're talking about, and Startsy's short notes about how you like to work. Not your name: Startsy keeps it on your phone and sends a stand-in ("[name]") instead. Not your other tasks.
Where it goesThe same as Break it down: MCPOTA's Startsy server on Cloudflare passes it to Anthropic's Claude AI, which writes Startsy's reply and, when ready, the task's steps.
What is keptMCPOTA does not store or log conversations, photos or file text. Anthropic deletes them within 30 days and does not train on them. On your phone, Startsy keeps your name and up to 30 short notes it learned (for example "has a clothes hamper"); it never notes health details, other people's names, addresses or money. See or delete them in Settings → Talking with Startsy. Startsy's voice is your phone's own text-to-speech.
GuardrailsStartsy sticks to starting and finishing tasks. It isn't a friend, therapist or medical service and doesn't treat anything. If someone mentions being in crisis, it points to the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988 in the US) or 911.

Update check (Android test builds only). iPhone test builds come from Apple's TestFlight app, which handles their updates; Startsy for iPhone has no update check of its own. On Android, if you tap Allow on the "Check for updates?" screen and enter a tester access code, each time you open Startsy it asks MCPOTA's builds server whether a newer test build is posted:

What is sentYour tester access code and a request for Startsy's latest test build, and, if you tap "Update now", a download of it. No tasks, no name, no account.
Where it goesMCPOTA's builds server, which runs on Cloudflare, checks the code and fetches the build from MCPOTA's private GitHub repository. Cloudflare sees your internet (IP) address, as any website does, and uses it briefly to limit how many requests one network can make. GitHub does not see your phone.
What is keptMCPOTA's builds server stores and logs nothing. The access code is saved only in Startsy on your phone. The downloaded file is checked, handed to Android's installer (which asks you to confirm) and then deleted. No old builds are kept on your phone.
How to stopSettings (⚙) → "Update check is on" → tap to turn it off. The store versions of Startsy will not have this check; the store handles updates.

Things you send yourself. Three Settings items hand information to another app on your phone, and only when you tap them:

  • Export my tasks gives a text copy of your tasks to the app you pick (email, notes, files). From there it follows that app's rules.
  • Send feedback opens your email app addressed to support@mcpota.com, with the Startsy and Android or iOS version numbers filled in. You see the whole email and nothing is sent until you tap Send. MCPOTA uses it only to answer you and improve Startsy.
  • Send a report first shows you the whole report file, exactly as it will be attached: the Startsy and Android or iOS version numbers, your phone's model name (for example "Pixel 9" or "iPhone"), and the app log described above (event names, numbers and times only; no tasks, steps, names, speech or photos). If you tap Send a report, your own email app opens addressed to support@mcpota.com with the file attached. You can add a note, remove the file, or not send it; nothing is sent until you tap Send in your email app. Startsy doesn't upload it anywhere itself; it goes by email through your email provider. MCPOTA uses it only to find and fix problems, keeps it with your email, and deletes it on request (privacy@mcpota.com).

Nothing else leaves your phone. Startsy has no ads, analytics, crash reporting or tracking.

Children

Startsy is not directed at children under 13.

Changes and contact

When this policy changes, the effective date above changes and the app's release notes say what changed. Questions: privacy@mcpota.com.
