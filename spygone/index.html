Behaviour, not guesswork

Most privacy tools tell you what an app could do. Spygone records what it actually did — when, how often, and who it talked to afterwards.

🔴 Findings you can act on

Every app is rated from what it did in the background. Red means it's sending or collecting your data right now; amber means worth a look; everything else stays quiet.

  • Background location, clipboard, contacts, SMS, photos, mic and camera use
  • Uploads while you weren't using the app, measured per app
  • Advertising and analytics services contacted while closed
  • Plain-English "How it could be misused" — by a company and by an attacker

🕒 A timeline for every app

See exactly what an app did and when. Spygone links a sensitive access to the network traffic that followed it — like a weather app reading your location and then contacting an ad network within seconds.

  • At a glance: opens, background accesses, trackers and data sent
  • Every access and every server it contacted, newest first
  • Access → network links within two minutes

🛠 Fix it in one tap — and undo it in one tap

Spygone shows exactly what it will change before it changes anything: restrict the battery, block background data, or remove one permission. The app keeps working while you use it.

  • Clear consent screen for every change
  • Undo any fix, any time
  • Before / after counts in your reports, so you can see it worked

↩ Fix check: catches fixes that get undone

App updates, reinstalls and system updates can quietly switch a permission back on. Spygone checks every fix and tells you when one was reversed, with Re-apply or Leave it.

  • Checks permissions, background data, battery and disabled apps
  • Explains the likely reason in plain English
  • Re-applying is part of the original fix

📊 Dashboard

Today at a glance: privacy risks, background accesses, trackers contacted and data sent. The full version keeps 7 days of history.

  • Most active apps while closed
  • Background data sent, per app
  • Tracker domains and who contacted them

📜 Privacy-policy grades

Each app's privacy policy is graded A to F under MCPOTA's published rules, with the policy's own words quoted for every point. Selling your data or sharing it for ads without an opt-out counts most.

  • Grades are MCPOTA's analysis of quoted policy text, not findings about a company
  • ⚖ Possible policy breach: when an app does something its policy says it never does
  • Built into the app, so looking an app up never leaves your phone

🕵 Spyware check

Separate from behaviour: a check of the access spyware, stalkerware, banking malware and scam apps rely on, plus a match against a public list of known stalkerware. Apps installed from a file are rated one step higher. See everything it checks →

  • Known stalkerware and monitoring apps, by signing certificate or package name
  • Screen readers, notification and text-message readers, phone managers
  • Remote-access apps used in fake bank and tech-support calls

Questions

Can it find Pegasus or other government-grade spyware?

Not from the phone app. That kind of spyware hides below the level any app can see. Spygone checks for the access ordinary spyware, stalkerware and scam apps need, and matches apps against a public list of known stalkerware. A forensic check of a phone backup on a computer, the method researchers use for Pegasus, is what Spygone Pro (in development) is for.

What should I do if it finds stalkerware?

Think about your safety before removing it: removing it can alert whoever installed it, and it may be evidence. Spygone shows a safety note and links to stopstalkerware.org, which has guidance and support contacts. It never removes stalkerware in one tap.

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, differently. iOS doesn't let apps see other apps, so Spygone for iPhone reads the App Privacy Report you share to it and gives you a guided Phone check of the settings stalkerware and scams use. It's in testing on TestFlight. More about the iPhone version →

Does it need root?

No. Basic monitoring works out of the box. The detailed background records and one-tap fixes use Android's Wireless debugging, set up once from the phone itself.

Will it slow my phone or drain the battery?

The VPN only looks at DNS lookups — tiny messages — and passes everything else straight through. Everything else runs on Android records the phone already keeps.

Does it work with another VPN?

Android allows one VPN at a time. If you use another VPN, the DNS part pauses; the access records still work with advanced access.

What if a finding is wrong?

Hide it, and optionally send a false-alarm report to support so the detection can be improved. Hidden findings can be shown again from ⚙ → Hidden findings.

What happens when the trial ends?

Monitoring stops and you can still undo any fix. Subscribe to keep watching.

Which phones are supported?

Android 10 and newer (advanced access needs Android 11 or newer for Wireless debugging). Tested on Samsung Galaxy phones.
