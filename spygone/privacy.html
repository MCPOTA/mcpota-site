MCPOTA Spygone Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026 · App ID com.mcpota.spotsy · Published by MCPOTA (mcpota.com) · Arx Dominii Privati

This policy covers the MCPOTA Spygone app for Android ("Spygone", "the app"; formerly called MCPOTA Privacy Watch). The iPhone version, now in testing, works differently and is covered in Spygone for iPhone. "We" and "us" mean MCPOTA, its publisher.

The short version
  • Spygone runs entirely on your phone. There is no account, no sign-in, and no Spygone server.
  • We never sell your data. The app doesn't collect or upload anything on its own; we receive only what you choose to email us, and only when you tap Send.
  • There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking tools in the app.
  • Something leaves your phone only when you choose to send it, for example by tapping Email report or Send logs to MCPOTA support (Android), or Send to MCPOTA for review (iPhone). You see the email before it's sent.

What the app does

Spygone shows you which apps on your phone access your location, microphone, camera, contacts, text messages, call log, calendar, clipboard and photos, which internet addresses they look up, and how much data they send and receive in the background. It reports what was observed, not what an app did with what it accessed: it never sees the content of app traffic. It then helps you change permissions or remove apps you don't want.

For apps MCPOTA has reviewed, the app also shows what the app's privacy policy allows the company to do with your data, with the policy's own words. Grades are MCPOTA's analysis of the policy's quoted text under published rules, not a finding about the company's conduct. The whole policy database ships inside the app, so looking an app up never contacts anyone. Where a reviewed policy makes a specific promise, such as that the app doesn't access your microphone, Spygone compares that promise with what it observed on your phone and shows a possible policy breach if they don't match. That comparison also happens only on your phone; nothing about it is sent anywhere.

While monitoring, Spygone can show alerts as notifications on your phone when an app you installed accesses something sensitive while you're not using it, sends data shortly after, or does something its reviewed privacy policy says it doesn't. Alerts are made on your phone from the same information described below; nothing about them is sent anywhere. You choose which kinds alert in Settings → Notifications → Alerts, and Android's notification settings can turn them off.

The app records that an app used a permission or contacted an address, and when. Spygone records how much each app sends, not what it sends. It does not read the content of your messages, calls, calendar, contacts, photos, files, clipboard, notifications, recordings or web pages. It does not read motion sensors either; apps can read those without a permission, and Android doesn't record which app did, so Spygone can't show it.

The local VPN

Android shows a key icon while Spygone is monitoring because the app uses Android's VPN feature. This VPN:

  • runs on your phone only, and does not route your traffic through any outside server;
  • handles only DNS lookups (the step where an app turns a name like example.com into an address). All your other traffic goes directly to the internet as usual;
  • passes each lookup on to your network's own DNS servers, the same servers your phone uses without Spygone, and nowhere else. They see the name being looked up, just as they would without Spygone;
  • cannot see the content of web pages or app traffic.

Optional advanced access

Some features need a higher level of access that Android only grants through Wireless debugging (or the Shizuku app). If you set this up, Spygone connects to your own phone over that local connection. It uses the connection to:

  • read permission-use history;
  • refresh Android's per-app network counters every 30 seconds while monitoring, so it can record how much each app sent and when (amounts only);
  • make changes you ask for and agree to (see the next section), and switch Usage access on for Spygone itself;
  • run Banking mode (see the next section).

Nothing obtained this way leaves your phone.

When information leaves your phone

Only when you take one of these actions:

  • Email report: the app opens your email app with the report attached. The report lists app names, domains and activity from the period you chose. You choose the recipients and press Send yourself. After that, the email is handled by your email provider and the people you send it to.
  • Send logs to MCPOTA support: after telling you what's in it, the app opens your email app with a log attached, addressed to support@mcpota.com. It contains your phone model and Android version, the app's status, the list of apps on your phone (package names, whether each came with the phone, and which store installed it) and which of them accessed the microphone, camera, location, contacts, texts, call log, calendar or other data and when, background data use, how much each app sent and when (amounts only), fixes made, and the error and crash logs. If advanced access is on, it also includes Android's recent records of microphone use, the camera service's log of when the camera was opened and closed, and Android system log lines filtered to Spygone, crashes and the VPN. It contains no messages, photos, passwords, call content or browsing history. You read the email and press Send yourself. MCPOTA stores the log privately, uses it only to diagnose and fix problems (including an automated diagnosis by Anthropic's Claude AI, under terms that don't allow it to be used for training), never copies your email address alongside it, and deletes it within 30 days.
  • Report a false alarm: see "False-alarm reports" below.
  • Upload logs (test builds only, not the Google Play version): opens a GitHub page in your browser where you choose whether to upload a troubleshooting log for review.
  • Copy: copying logs puts them on your clipboard so you can paste them where you choose.
  • Check for updates (test builds only, not the Google Play version): after you allow it and type a tester access code, the app asks MCPOTA's builds server (run on Cloudflare) whether a newer test build exists each time you open it, and downloads it only when you tap Update now. It sends the access code and the request, nothing the app recorded. Cloudflare sees your internet address, as any website does. Android's own installer asks you to confirm. The downloaded file is deleted the next time the app opens. Turn it off in ⚙ → Turn off the update check.

False-alarm reports

If you think something was flagged by mistake, you can tap Not a concern? Report a false alarm. Spygone then stops flagging it on your phone. If you also choose Hide and share, your email app opens with a short report addressed to support@mcpota.com. It contains what was flagged and facts about that app: its name, package, version, where it was installed from, the permissions it requested and was granted, and its services, plus your phone model and Android version. It also includes the lines Spygone showed for that finding: which kinds of access happened, how often and when, how much the app sent, how many web addresses it looked up, and the names of any ad or analytics services it contacted. It contains no messages, photos, contacts or browsing, and you see it before pressing Send.

We use these reports only to improve how Spygone detects problems. We may review them with the help of AI tools for that purpose. We don't use them for anything else, and we delete them once they've been reviewed.

Subscriptions

Purchases open when Spygone launches on Google Play. If you subscribe to Spygone Pro, the purchase is handled by Google Play. We never see your payment card or billing details. Google Play tells the app only whether your subscription is active. Google's handling of purchases is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

What we never do

  • We never sell or rent data, including anything you choose to send us.
  • We don't show ads, and we include no advertising or analytics code.
  • We don't build profiles of you or the apps you use.
  • We don't upload your activity, contacts, location or any other personal information.

Your choices

  • Stop monitoring at any time from the app, or by turning off the VPN in Android settings.
  • Delete recorded activity with ⚙ → Clear recorded events, and logs with ⚙ → Logs → Clear.
  • Undo any fix from the app (the app's timeline, or the Fixed list) whenever you like.
  • Turn off Usage access or advanced access in Android settings whenever you like.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all of its data.

Children

Spygone is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age.

Security

Except reports and troubleshooting logs you save to Downloads, your data stays in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. The app sends nothing to us on its own, so there is no server copy of what it records. Emails you choose to send us are kept privately and deleted as described above.

Spygone for iPhone

iPhones don't let one app see what other apps do, so the iPhone version has no monitoring, no VPN and no fixes it makes itself. It reads the App Privacy Report that iOS keeps (Settings → Privacy & Security → App Privacy Report) when you save it and share it to Spygone, or open a saved copy. That report lists, for the last 7 days, which apps accessed your camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos and similar, and which internet domains they contacted. It does not contain what apps sent.

  • Read on your phone, in memory. The report is read on your phone and shown while the app is open. Its contents are not stored: the copy iOS hands to the app is deleted as soon as it has been read.
  • What is stored on your phone: whether you finished setup, the date you said you turned App Privacy Report on, the date of the last report you shared (its newest entry, so the app can tell you when it is 7 or more days old), whether the weekly reminder is on, which screen to show after a report is read, and when you last tapped Later on the out-of-date message. These stay until you delete the app.
  • Weekly reminder (optional): if you turn it on and allow notifications, a notification scheduled on your phone reminds you once a week to share a fresh report. Turn it off in the app or in iOS Settings → Notifications.
  • Summary, Details, Dashboard and timelines are worked out on your phone from the report you shared, while the app is open. They are not stored; close the app or share another report and they are gone.
  • Share report (optional): when you tap Share report, the app makes a PDF of the findings on your phone and opens the iOS share sheet. You choose where it goes (for example Mail, Messages or Files); nothing is sent unless you pick a destination. The app writes the PDF to its temporary folder only so the share sheet can hand it on, and deletes it when the share sheet closes (or the next time the app opens). It does not keep a copy.
  • Send to MCPOTA for review (optional): if you'd like MCPOTA to look at an app's privacy policy, tap Send to MCPOTA for review. You pick which apps to include (none is picked for you). The app then shows you exactly what will be in the email, and only after you confirm does it open a ready-made email in your own Mail app, addressed to reports@mcpota.com. Nothing is sent until you tap Send in Mail, and you can change or delete anything in the email first. If Mail isn't set up, the app shows the address and lets you share the same files to another email app with the iOS share sheet.
    • What's in it: for each app you picked, its name and iPhone app ID, the lines Spygone showed for it (which kinds of access iOS recorded and how many times, and how many domains it contacted, naming the most-contacted ones), and MCPOTA's policy grade for it with the quoted policy clauses and what they mean. It also has the dates the App Privacy Report covers, the Spygone version, the date the email was made, and a note that this is a snapshot derived from Apple's App Privacy Report cross-referenced with MCPOTA's policy grades. These are attached as a PDF and a JSON (data) file, with a short list of the apps in the email itself.
    • What's not in it: the App Privacy Report file itself, its raw entries, anything about apps you didn't pick, and nothing else from your phone. Your email address and name reach us only because the email comes from your account.
    • On your phone: the attachments are handed to Mail without being saved. If you use the share-sheet route instead, the app writes the two files to its temporary folder so the share sheet can hand them on, and deletes them when you close that screen (or the next time the app opens).
    • How MCPOTA uses it: only to review the privacy-policy concerns you reported, for example to check or update our analysis of an app's policy. We may review it with the help of AI tools for that purpose, under terms that don't allow it to be used for training. We don't sell or share it, don't use it for advertising, and don't add your email address to any list. We keep it until the review is finished and then delete it; write to privacy@mcpota.com to have it deleted sooner. Once you send it, the email is also handled by your email provider.
  • Phone check: a guided list of iPhone settings to look at yourself (profiles, certificates, VPN, keyboards, signed-in devices, location sharing, trusted numbers). The app can't see those settings. Your answers stay on that screen only; they aren't stored or sent, and they're cleared when you leave it.
  • Nothing else leaves your phone. The iPhone version has no account, ads, analytics or tracking. Apart from what you choose to send with Share report or Send to MCPOTA for review, it sends nothing to MCPOTA or anyone else. Tapping Read the full policy on an app's page opens the company's website in your browser.
  • Policy grades are MCPOTA's analysis of quoted privacy-policy text, as described above. The reviews ship inside the app, so looking an app up never leaves your phone. Reviews are made from the privacy policy linked on the company's Google Play listing and matched to the same company's iPhone app; a company's iPhone and Android apps usually share one policy.
